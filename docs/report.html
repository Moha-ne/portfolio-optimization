Serenity & Velocity

Project report - Mohamed Moussa Moha - November 2026

The brief: a client wants to invest $2 billion and the asset manager offers two funds. Serenity is for cautious clients, Velocity for clients who accept more risk. Each fund holds 25 assets (US stocks and ETFs). The weights come from the Markowitz model seen in class, and the risk is measured with beta, alpha and Value at Risk. All figures use three years of daily prices from Yahoo Finance, from November 2023 to November 2026.

Risk-free rate: 13-week US Treasury bill (^IRX). Beta and alpha come from the course script, which uses an equal-weight version of each fund.

Serenity does what it is meant to do: its volatility is about half that of the S&P 500 and its beta is low, so a 10% move in the market moves the fund by about 2.6%. The worst 10-day loss expected at 99% is $62M, or 3.1% of the fund. Velocity is a bit more volatile than the index and follows the market more closely (beta 1.12), but it earned much more over the period. Its 10-day VaR is about twice Serenity's, 6.3% of the fund.

Both Sharpe ratios are close to 2, which is high. Part of this comes from a very good three years for some of these stocks (Micron, Palantir and Carvana were multiplied by 10 to 12). A Sharpe ratio measured on the past is not a forecast.

How the funds were built

  1. Universe. Serenity mixes bond funds (SHY, IEF, TIP, MUB, VCSH), gold and agriculture (GLD, DBA), two strategies with little link to the stock market (DBMF, MNA) and defensive companies: consumer staples, health care, insurance, utilities, exchanges and a few large tech names. Velocity started from 58 liquid candidates (tech, finance, industry, energy, gold); I kept the 25 that gave the best Sharpe ratio together. No crypto: it trades on weekends, which breaks the alignment with stock market days.
  2. Z-scores. Each asset gets a score from its momentum, volatility and beta: 0.5 z(momentum) - 0.3 z(volatility) - 0.2 z(beta) for Serenity, and 0.6 / 0.2 / 0.2 for Velocity to give more weight to momentum.
  3. Markowitz. 10,000 random portfolios are simulated from the mean returns and the covariance matrix of the 25 assets. The one with the highest Sharpe ratio is kept. For Serenity, swapping 10 assets (Merck among them) raised the Sharpe ratio from 1.53 to 1.97 while keeping the four bond funds and a volatility near 8%.
  4. CAPM. Beta and alpha of each fund against the S&P 500.
  5. Value at Risk. Parametric VaR at 99% on $2 billion, from 1 to 10 days.

Limits

  1. The weights are optimised on the same three years used to measure the results. Testing them on a later period would give a more honest Sharpe ratio.
  2. 10,000 random portfolios get close to the best mix but do not find it exactly, and random weights almost never put an asset at zero. An optimiser (scipy.optimize) would do better.
  3. The parametric VaR assumes normal returns. The histograms show more extreme days than a normal law, so the real 99% loss can be larger. A historical VaR would be a good check.
  4. Beta and alpha are measured on an equal-weight portfolio, not on the optimal weights.
